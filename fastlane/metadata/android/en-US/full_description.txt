Parvaz (Persian for "flight") helps you open websites your network blocks. It sends your browser's traffic through a relay: a small Google Apps Script program on a Google account, set up by you or by a technical helper you trust.

To start, you need an access link from your helper. It begins with parvaz://. Tap or paste the link, install a certificate once in Android Settings, and tap the PARVAZ button. The certificate tells your phone to trust Parvaz for your browser's secure connections. Chrome accepts it, and Brave, Edge and Vivaldi should too. Parvaz opens in Persian, with English and Danish in its settings.

The person who runs your relay, and Google, can read everything that passes through it, cookies and passwords included. Use Parvaz to read public pages. Do not sign in to accounts you cannot afford to lose, unless you trust the relay's owner completely.

Parvaz needs Android 11 or later and a 64-bit ARM phone. It installs on Android 7 to 10 but cannot connect there.

What does not work: apps other than browsers, such as banking, messaging and streaming apps, do not accept the certificate. Firefox is not tested. Pages load slowly, daily quotas limit use, and WebSockets are not supported.
